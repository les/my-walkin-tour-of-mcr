# My Walking Tour of Manchester

1. [Curry Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry_Mile): A name earned from the large number of restaurants, takeaways, and kebab houses specialising in the cuisines of South Asia and the Middle East thought to be the largest concentration of South Asian restaurants in the UK.
2. [Kimpton Clocktower Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimpton_Clocktower_Hotel): Built over 41 years (1891–1932) for the [Refuge Assurance Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refuge_Assurance_Company) by three successive architects including [Alfred Waterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Waterhouse). Workplace rules once segregated men and women at lunch and required women to reapply for their jobs if they married.
3. [Manchester Central Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Central_Library): A Pantheon-inspired [rotunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotunda) opened by King George V in 1934. Its music collection contains previously unknown [Vivaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Vivaldi) violin sonatas — autographed by the composer — now called _The Manchester Sonatas_. Opposite stands [Rise up, Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rise_up,_Women), a statue of [Emmeline Pankhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmeline_Pankhurst) celebrating Manchester's role in the [suffragette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragette) movement.
4. [Midland Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Hotel,_Manchester): Where Charles Rolls met Henry Royce in 1904, leading to the formation of [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Motor_Cars). Allegedly coveted by [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) as a possible Nazi headquarters in Britain — American intelligence speculated the area around the [town hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Town_Hall) was spared from bombing during the [Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Blitz) so as not to destroy it.
5. [Rochdale Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochdale_Canal): The first trans-Pennine canal (1804), descending through nine locks from Ancoats to Castlefield. Passes along [Canal Street](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_Street_(Manchester)>), whose sign is often defaced to read _anal treet_.
6. [Science and Industry Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_Industry_Museum): Built on the world's first inter-city passenger railway station — the terminus of the [Liverpool and Manchester Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_and_Manchester_Railway) (1830). Also home to a replica of the [Manchester Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Baby), the world's first stored-program computer (1948).
7. [John Rylands Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rylands_Research_Institute_and_Library): The first public building in Manchester to be lit by electricity — generating its own power on-site until 1950.
8. [The Hidden Gem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hidden_Gem): The first Catholic church to be opened in Manchester following the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) (Henry VIII's 16th-century break with Rome that banned Catholic worship for over 200 years).
9. [Manchester Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Art_Gallery): [The Sirens and Ulysses (1837)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sirens_and_Ulysses) for which [William Etty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Etty) sketched real corpses in a mortuary. Hidden in storage for over 150 years after self-destructing due to an experimental paint technique, it was restored and redisplayed in 2010.
10. [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Manchester): The second largest Chinatown in the UK. Beneath the nearby [Guardian telephone exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_telephone_exchange) lies a [Cold War nuclear bunker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_telephone_exchange) built 1949–1954 with NATO funding — over four miles of tunnels constructed by Polish workers who couldn't speak English, presumably to maintain secrecy. [Wong Wong Bakery](https://maps.app.goo.gl/j13JtTFaJjRcrJPq6) is also worth a visit for delicious Chinese pastries and cakes (๑´ڡ`๑)
11. [Bridgewater Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgewater_Canal): Britain's first true artificial canal (1761), built for the [Duke of Bridgewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Egerton,_3rd_Duke_of_Bridgewater) to move coal from [Worsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worsley). Halved the price of coal in Manchester and sparked [Canal Mania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_Mania) — its terminus at [Castlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlefield) is where it meets the Rochdale Canal. The [Manchester Ship Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Ship_Canal) replaced it in the 1890s when it couldn't handle the volume; the [Barton Swing Aqueduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Swing_Aqueduct) now carries one over the other.
12. [Alan Turing Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing_Memorial): Commemorates the father of modern computing and Enigma codebreaker.
13. [Ancoats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancoats): The world's first steam-powered industrial suburb — and [birthplace of the ice cream cone](https://unlockmanchester.com/guide-articles/ancoats). In 1910, [Avro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro) set up an aircraft factory in [Brownsfield Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsfield_Mill); soldiers from Ancoats serving in France during WWI reportedly recognised planes flying overhead that had been built in their own neighbourhood.
14. [Old Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Mill,_Manchester): The world's oldest surviving urban steam-powered cotton spinning factory. Completed in 1798 as part of the [Murrays' Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrays%27_Mills) complex, powered by a [Boulton and Watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulton_and_Watt) engine — its narrow six-storey brick form "came to typify the Manchester cotton mill".
15. [Sanitary Street](https://ilovemanchester.com/ancoats-sanitary-street): Later renamed _Anita Street_ by removing letters from the street sign, was the first Manchester street to have individual toilets and sinks in each house (1897). Adjacent Victoria Square was Manchester's first-ever council housing scheme (1894) replacing vast swathes of slums on Oldham Road.
16. [National Football Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Museum): The world's largest football collection — key items include the [1966 replica Jules Rimet Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Rimet_Trophy#Replicas), secretly made by the FA after [the original was stolen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_theft_of_the_Jules_Rimet_Trophy) and what England's players actually paraded at the World Cup Final, and [L.S. Lowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._S._Lowry)'s original [_Going to the Match_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_to_the_Match).
17. [Chetham's Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetham%27s_Library): The oldest free public reference library in the English-speaking world (1653). [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx) and [Friedrich Engels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Engels) met and worked here in 1845, studying economics texts — research that led to [_The Communist Manifesto_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Communist_Manifesto).
18. [Corn Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Exchange,_Manchester): A triangular [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) grain-trading hall (1903) with a glass dome. Pips nightclub, around back, was where [Joy Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Division) played their first gig under that name in 1978.
19. [The Old Wellington Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Wellington_Inn): Manchester's only surviving Tudor building (1552) was damaged by the [1996 IRA bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Manchester_bombing), then [dismantled and rebuilt 300 metres away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shambles_Square,_Manchester#Relocation) to form Shambles Square. _Duck or Grouse_.
20. [Hanging Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanging_Bridge): A 1421 sandstone bridge over a post-glacial ditch that formed Manchester's medieval defences — buried and forgotten, then rediscovered during demolition in 1880 and promptly covered up again. Residents once built privies overhanging the ditch that discharged directly into it, despite repeated official bans.

## Breweries & Pubs

1. [Peveril of the Peak](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peveril_of_the_Peak_(pub)>): c.1820, Grade II listed. Green tile exterior, Art Nouveau stained glass, and original bell pushes — [CAMRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Real_Ale) rates its interior as being of _"outstanding national historic importance"_.
2. [Sir Ralph Abercromby](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Ralph_Abercromby_(pub)>): One of the few structures still standing from the time of the [Peterloo Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterloo_Massacre) — wounded were brought inside for treatment. Thought to be the inspiration for the pub in [_Life on Mars_](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_on_Mars_(British_TV_series)>).
3. [The Britons Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britons_Protection): c.1806, Grade II listed. Named for its use as an army recruiting venue. Unconfirmed reports say Peterloo wounded were laid on the bar. Over 300 whiskies.
4. [The Circus Tavern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Tavern,_Manchester): 1842, Grade II listed. Claims to be the smallest bar in Europe — [George Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Best) was a regular.
5. [Sam's Chop House](https://web.archive.org/web/20121112172816/http://menmedia.co.uk/manchestereveningnews/news/s/1408770_back_at_his_local_statue_of_ls_lowry_installed_at_the_bar_of_sams_chop_house?all_comments=1): c.1869 Victorian chophouse. [L.S. Lowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._S._Lowry) ate here almost every day for decades; a life-sized bronze of him now sits at the bar.
6. [City Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Arms): A Georgian townhouse turned ale house, Grade II listed. Layers nearly 250 years of Manchester drinking history — 18th-century brickwork, Art Deco fireplaces, and 1970s Tetley's branding — and sits beside the equally listed [Vine Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine_Inn).
7. [The Mitre Hotel](https://mitrehotel.co.uk/about-us/): One of Manchester's earliest hotels (1815 — the year of [Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo)), named for the bishop's [mitre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitre) due to its neighbour, the Cathedral.
8. [Marble Arch Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Arch_Inn): 1888, Grade II listed. Polished granite columns, mosaic floors, and glazed brick interior — on CAMRA's [National Inventory of Historic Pub Interiors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Inventory_of_Historic_Pub_Interiors). [Marble Brewery](https://marblebeers.com/) was founded in its back room in 1997.
9. [Cloudwater Brew Co](https://cloudwaterbrew.co/): Internationally acclaimed modern brewery, frequently ranked among the world's best. The anchor of Manchester's "Beer Mile" near [Piccadilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Piccadilly_station).
10. [Track Brewing Co](https://www.trackbrewing.co/): Highly regarded modern brewery on the Beer Mile, next to Cloudwater.
